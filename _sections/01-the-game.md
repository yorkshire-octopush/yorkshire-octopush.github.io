**Octopush is the British name for underwater hockey.** Two teams of six players, wearing a mask, snorkel and fins, try to push a heavy puck along the bottom of a swimming pool and into the other team's goal.

Players hold their breath, dive to the bottom and play the puck with a short stick, then swim back up to breathe while a team-mate takes over. Nobody can stay down for long, so passing and teamwork matter more than size or strength. It is fast, skilful and great fun, and it builds swimming fitness and breath-holding without anyone noticing they are training.

The game was invented in Britain in the 1950s and is now played all over the world, with national and international competitions for juniors as well as adults.

<div class="cards" markdown="1">

<div class="card" markdown="1">
### The puck
Made of lead and about the size of an ice-hockey puck, so it stays on the bottom of the pool.
</div>

<div class="card" markdown="1">
### The stick
A short stick, about the length of your forearm, held in one hand. Teams play in black or white.
</div>

<div class="card" markdown="1">
### The kit
Mask, snorkel and fins, plus a padded glove to protect your hand and a hat with ear guards.
</div>

<div class="card" markdown="1">
### The goals
Long metal trays at each end of the pool floor. Score by pushing or flicking the puck into the tray.
</div>
